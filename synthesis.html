iCLIP and the Evolution of Additive Manufacturing

Injection continuous liquid interface production (iCLIP) is one of the most promising emerging technologies in additive manufacturing with its increase in speed, resolution, and scalability suited for mass production in medical and industrial applications. Lipkowitz et al. (2022) highlight the importance of iCLIP as an emerging platform, demonstrating "increases in the maximum achievable print rates determined to be between 5- and 10-fold" (p. 4). Their work not only brings attention to this emerging technology, but also explains its numerous benefits over current leaders in the additive manufacturing field. Lipkowitz further shows the mechanical strengths of the produced parts from iCLIP, matching and in some cases exceeding the force ratings of its CLIP predecessor, particularly in three-point bending and uniaxial compression tests.

Similarly to Lipkowitz's earlier findings, newer research by Lipkowitz et al. (2024) further develops the technology, creating a contrast to the traditional CLIP systems, showing improvements of over 42% faster and 75% fewer support structures (p. 15). By modeling the Stefan forces, Lipkowitz et al. (2024) built a system that eliminates the slower resin pumping required by previous technologies. Further modeling of the system allows for a better understanding of the printer as a whole. Simulation, Lipkowitz concludes, will increase ease of scalability and "hasten [iCLIP's] adoption in real-world manufacturing settings" (p. 1). Lipkowitz further strengthens previous findings about this technology, building on its promising future in various additive manufacturing fields.

Coates et al. (2024) focus on the resolution of iCLIP and the advantages the technology brings to micro-manufacturing, adding onto previous advantages found by Lipkowitz. Through iCLIP, Coates et al. were able to circumvent internal void detail loss due to overcuring. With this development, iCLIP would be one of the first technologies of its kind to create "high-resolution microsystem devices such as vascular beds and microfluidic-backed microneedles" (p. 2). Together, these studies position iCLIP as a scalable, high-resolution additive manufacturing process with strong potential in medical and industrial applications.
